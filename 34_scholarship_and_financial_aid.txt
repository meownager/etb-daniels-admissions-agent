SCHOLARSHIP AND FINANCIAL AID

All applicants to Daniels School graduate programs are automatically considered for merit-based scholarships as part of the admissions review. No separate scholarship application is required. Scholarship decisions are made alongside or shortly after the admission decision, and award amounts are communicated with or following the acceptance letter.

Merit scholarships are awarded based on a combination of factors including academic record, test scores if submitted, professional experience and achievement, and the strength of the application overall. There is no formula — the awards are discretionary and reflect the admissions committee's assessment of the applicant's potential and fit.

Applying by the priority deadline, typically in January for most programs, maximizes scholarship eligibility. Applicants who apply after the priority deadline may still be considered for financial aid, but the amount of available funding decreases as the cycle progresses and cohort seats fill.

Beyond merit scholarships, there are additional ways to offset the cost of attendance. Students may be eligible for graduate assistantships through research centers at the Daniels School. For example, the Krenicki Center for Business Analytics and Machine Learning periodically offers paid positions for students to work on analytics and consulting projects. These positions provide both financial support and professional experience. Students should inquire with the admissions or program office about current availability.

The Purdue University Division of Financial Aid administers federal student loans and other need-based aid. Students who need financing beyond scholarships and assistantships can explore federal loan options through this office. International students who are not eligible for federal aid often use private education loan providers such as Prodigy Finance or MPOWER for program financing.

For a prospect asking whether they can afford the program: the most important advice is to apply by the January priority deadline and let the merit review process run. Scholarship awards can meaningfully reduce the net cost, and the advisors in the financial aid office can help model the financing options that remain after any scholarship is applied.
