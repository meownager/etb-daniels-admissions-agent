MASTER OF BUSINESS AND TECHNOLOGY (MBT) — CAREER OUTCOMES

The Master of Business and Technology at the Daniels School is a one-year program available in residential and online formats. It is STEM-designated. The MBT is not a standard MBA and it is not a technology degree — it is a deliberate fusion of both, designed for the growing market of professionals who need to lead at the intersection of business strategy and emerging technology. In 2023, nearly 31,000 employers competed to fill over 400,000 positions requiring both core business skills and STEM training and experience. The MBT is designed specifically for that role.

The program builds on MBA-level content — finance, accounting, marketing, operations, strategic management — and goes deeper on technology strategy, computational business intelligence, and how emerging technologies reshape business models. A graduate who has completed the MBT described the experience as: the finance, marketing, and accounting of an MBA, plus the strategic and computational skills to actually drive innovation. Another noted that the program provides strategic thinking for a corporation rather than just linear thinking — with specific emphasis on how to drive strategic insights using data and technology.

Typical career destinations for MBT graduates include Technology Manager, Digital Transformation Lead, Product Manager, Business Technology Consultant, Operations and Technology Analyst, and over the long term, technology leadership roles including Chief Technology Officer. Industries include technology companies, manufacturing firms with technology transformation agendas, consulting firms specializing in digital strategy, healthcare technology, and AI-driven companies. Symbotic, an AI-driven warehouse automation company, is a representative example of the employer type.

The job market outlook for this profile is projected to grow 6 to 10 percent through 2032, but the more important context is the structural gap: most business programs produce general managers, and most technology programs produce engineers. Very few programs produce people who can do both — lead a business unit and understand the technology shaping it. That gap is what the MBT fills, and it is why employers specifically compete for this profile.

The online version of the MBT is also available, with an Indianapolis Experience option for students who want periodic in-person engagement while maintaining flexibility. For a tech or engineering professional who feels capped without business credentials, or for someone who wants to lead digital transformation rather than just participate in it, the MBT is the primary recommendation at Daniels.
