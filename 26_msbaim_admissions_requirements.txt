MS BUSINESS ANALYTICS AND INFORMATION MANAGEMENT (MSBAIM) — ADMISSIONS REQUIREMENTS

The MSBAIM is a one-year residential program. Applicants typically come from quantitative or technical undergraduate backgrounds including STEM fields, economics, computer science, mathematics, statistics, and business analytics. Students from other fields with strong quantitative coursework are also considered.

GMAT and GRE test scores are not required for applicants with a cumulative undergraduate GPA of 3.0 or higher. The admissions committee will look for evidence of quantitative aptitude in the application through coursework and academic record. Test scores are encouraged for scholarship consideration and may strengthen a competitive application. If test scores are submitted, they become part of the application and are considered in the admission decision.

Work experience is not required for the MSBAIM. Many students enter directly from undergraduate study. The program's strong placement outcomes reflect both the quality of the curriculum and the employer demand for this skill set regardless of prior work experience.

Deadlines follow a priority structure. The January priority deadline maximizes scholarship eligibility. Applications received after the priority deadline in March or May may still be considered for financial aid based on available funding, but priority consideration is not guaranteed for late applicants.

The Pathway Program is available for this degree. Purdue undergraduates and select partner school graduates can apply through the Pathway Program with reduced application requirements. TOEFL and IELTS are waived for U.S. graduates applying through this pathway.

For the application, the Statement of Purpose is particularly important for MSBAIM. The admissions committee wants to see a clear connection between the applicant's quantitative background, their career goals in analytics or data-driven roles, and why the MSBAIM specifically positions them to reach those goals. Vague statements about "wanting to work with data" are less compelling than specific career targets and a clear explanation of how the program's curriculum addresses a specific skill gap.
