OBJECTION RESPONSE — "I'M NOT READY" OR "I NEED MORE EXPERIENCE FIRST"

Underlying concern: The prospect doubts their own qualifications, is unclear on what "enough" experience looks like, or is genuinely at a stage where waiting makes sense.

How to respond: Clarify what each program actually requires, then ask what "ready" specifically means to them.

"That depends a lot on which program you're thinking about. For most of the specialized master's programs — MS Finance, MSBAIM, Supply Chain, Marketing, HR — there's no experience requirement at all. Many students come in directly from undergraduate study or within a year or two of graduating. These programs are designed as post-undergraduate credentials, not mid-career moves. For the MBA, the typical range is two to five years of experience, but there's no strict cutoff — the committee looks at the full picture. Can I ask what makes you feel you're not ready yet? Is it about qualifications, or more about timing in your life?"

If they identify a specific gap: Address it directly. For example, if they say "I don't have enough business knowledge," that is exactly what the One-Year MBA is designed for people without prior business study.

If they are genuinely earlier in their career and waiting is the right call: Be honest about it. "If you're in your first year of work and you're thinking about an MBA specifically, it probably makes sense to build another year or two of experience first — not because you won't get in, but because the MBA is more valuable when you have more professional context to apply it to. For a specialized master's, though, now is actually the ideal time."
