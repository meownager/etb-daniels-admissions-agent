OBJECTION RESPONSE — "I ALREADY HAVE EXPERIENCE — DO I REALLY NEED A DEGREE?"

Underlying concern: The prospect questions whether a degree adds value on top of their existing experience. They may feel they have learned more from work than they would from a classroom.

How to respond: Acknowledge the validity of experience, then distinguish what a degree adds that experience alone does not.

"Your experience is genuinely valuable — and it's actually what makes a graduate degree more useful at your stage, not less. Here's the thing: experience teaches you how to operate in the environment you're already in. A degree helps you move into environments that don't know you yet. If you're trying to get promoted within your current company, your track record speaks for itself. But if you want to move to a new company, change industries, or get into management at a level above where you've been, a lot of employers use the graduate degree as a filter — not because they doubt your capabilities, but because it's an efficient shortcut for sorting applications. The degree doesn't replace your experience. It makes your experience legible to people who haven't seen it directly."

Then ask: "Have you hit a ceiling you're trying to break through, or is this more of a proactive move?"

If they're in a comfortable position and not feeling blocked: "If things are going well and you're not feeling stuck, it's worth being honest about whether the timing makes sense. The programs will still be there in two years. But if you're starting to see peers with graduate degrees move past you into roles you want, that's usually when the investment starts to make clear sense."
