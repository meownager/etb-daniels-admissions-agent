MS GLOBAL SUPPLY CHAIN MANAGEMENT (MSGSCM) — ADMISSIONS REQUIREMENTS

The MSGSCM is a one-year residential program. Applicants come from a range of backgrounds including business, engineering, operations, logistics, and related fields. The program requires quantitative aptitude, and the admissions committee looks for evidence of this through coursework, professional experience, or test scores.

The preferred GMAT score for the MSGSCM is 585 or higher. The GRE is accepted in lieu of the GMAT, with a minimum preferred score of 290 on the combined verbal and quantitative sections. GMAT and GRE waivers are available under several conditions: applicants with four or more years of professional experience who demonstrate strong quantitative background, applicants with a graduate degree from an accredited U.S. institution, Purdue undergraduate students who have earned a 3.3 or above in specific quantitative coursework (MGMT 30500, MA 16010 or 16020, MA 16500 or 16600, or STAT 30301), and students who hold an MITx MicroMasters certificate in supply chain management. Waiver requests are submitted after beginning the application by contacting businessgradadmissions@purdue.edu.

Work experience is not required for admission. However, many MSGSCM students enter with some professional experience in supply chain, operations, engineering, or logistics. A limited number of strong applicants with little or no work experience are admitted each year, particularly those with significant co-op or internship experience.

Deadlines follow a priority structure. The January priority deadline maximizes scholarship eligibility. Applications after the priority deadline may still be considered but scholarship availability is reduced.

The Pathway Program is available for MSGSCM. Purdue undergraduates and select partner school graduates can apply with reduced requirements through this track.

For a prospective student with an engineering background and limited business coursework: the admissions committee specifically values engineering as strong preparation for supply chain management. A GMAT around 585 or evidence of strong quantitative performance in engineering coursework will make the application competitive.
