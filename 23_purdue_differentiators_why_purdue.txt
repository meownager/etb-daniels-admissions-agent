WHY PURDUE — PROGRAM DIFFERENTIATORS

When a prospect is comparing Purdue to other schools, the following points are the most substantive and defensible differentiators. Do not use generic prestige language. Use specific, factual claims.

The engineering heritage is the most distinctive structural advantage. Purdue is one of the top engineering universities in the United States, and this heritage runs through the business school in a way that most standalone business schools cannot replicate. Programs like the Integrated Business and Engineering undergraduate major, the Master of Business and Technology, and the MSGSCM are built at the business-engineering intersection with the full institutional weight of both colleges behind them. A company hiring for an operations leadership role, a supply chain strategy position, or a technology management role knows that a Purdue business graduate has had genuine contact with engineering rigor — not just a module on operations management.

Employer relationships are deep, not just broad. Many schools can claim that Fortune 500 companies recruit on campus. What is more meaningful is that specific companies return year after year and hire multiple graduates each cycle. Capital One hired 13 Daniels graduates in 2024-2025. Amazon hired 9. FedEx hired 6. EY hired 6. This kind of repeated, volume hiring indicates a genuine talent pipeline relationship, not a one-time campus visit.

The public university value is real. Comparable one-year master's programs at private business schools frequently cost $90,000 to $130,000 or more. Most Daniels specialized master's programs are in the range of $55,000 to $70,000. The placement rates and employer relationships are competitive with schools charging significantly more. For a prospect doing the math on ROI, Purdue's public university pricing is a genuine advantage, not a fallback.

The STEM designation across multiple programs is increasingly important. As employers in technology, finance, consulting, and manufacturing increasingly require analytical rigor, and as international students increasingly factor OPT eligibility into their school choice, the STEM designation on programs like the One-Year MBA, MBT, MSF, MSBAIM, and MSGSCM is a structural benefit that many peer schools do not offer across as broad a range of programs.

If a prospect asks how Purdue compares to a specific school, do not speculate or make comparative claims about the other school. Focus on what Purdue specifically offers and ask the prospect what matters most to them in making the choice.
