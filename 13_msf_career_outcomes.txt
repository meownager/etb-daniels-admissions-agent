MS FINANCE (MSF) — CAREER OUTCOMES

The MS Finance program at the Daniels School is a 10-month residential program designed for students targeting technical roles in investment management, capital markets, corporate finance, investment banking, and financial risk. It is a more specialized and quantitatively rigorous path than the MBA, suited to students who have identified finance as their specific domain and want depth rather than breadth.

Employment outcomes show 77 percent of MSF graduates finding full-time positions within six months. The average starting salary is $82,803, and the highest reported salary in the most recent cycle reached $155,000. Among graduates with confirmed job titles, 50 percent moved into Analyst roles, 28.57 percent into Associate roles, 7.14 percent into Consultant roles, and the remainder into other positions.

Top employers hiring MSF graduates include Goldman Sachs, JP Morgan Chase, Deutsche Bank, Morgan Stanley, Raymond James Financial, Capital One, Truist, Cerity Partners, StoneX, and TIAA. Graduates work primarily in investment banking, asset management, corporate treasury, insurance, and financial consulting. The average class GMAT score over the past five years has been approximately 630, and the average GRE has been 320 to 322, which gives a sense of the competitiveness of the cohort.

MSF students have access to the Purdue Finance Workshop and the Student Managed Venture Fund, both of which provide hands-on experience with real portfolios and market analysis. The curriculum is designed with CFA exam preparation in mind, meaning students pursuing the CFA designation benefit from alignment between program content and exam requirements.

The key comparison to make when recommending the MSF over the MBA is this: the MBA builds breadth across all business functions and is better suited for general management, consulting, and career switching. The MSF builds depth in finance and is better suited for someone who is certain they want to work in investment banking, asset management, or corporate finance, and who wants the technical credibility that finance employers specifically look for.
