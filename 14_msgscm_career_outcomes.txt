MS GLOBAL SUPPLY CHAIN MANAGEMENT (MSGSCM) — CAREER OUTCOMES

The MS Global Supply Chain Management program at the Daniels School is a one-year residential program designed for students and professionals interested in logistics, procurement, operations, and supply chain strategy. Purdue's engineering heritage gives this program a distinctive depth — students learn both the strategic and technical dimensions of supply chain management, which is not available at most pure business schools.

Employment outcomes are strong. 85 percent of MSGSCM graduates find full-time employment within six months of graduation. The average starting salary is $93,460, and the highest reported salary in the most recent cycle reached $180,000, the highest maximum of any specialized master's program at Daniels. The labor market context is favorable: supply chain roles are projected to grow 19 percent through 2033 according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics.

Job title distribution among recent graduates is notably balanced. Approximately 30 percent of graduates moved directly into Analyst roles, 30 percent into Manager roles, 15 percent into Planner roles, and the remainder into Specialist, Consultant, and Demand Planning positions. The fact that nearly a third of graduates enter at the Manager level directly from the program reflects both the seniority of some incoming students and the strength of employer relationships.

Top employers include FedEx with 6 hires, OMP with 4 hires, Foxconn with 3 hires, CNH Industrial with 2 hires, and single hires from Cummins, Carrier, P&G, GM, Nestle, The Andersons, Wintec Industries, and Yazaki. Graduates work across manufacturing, logistics, retail, aerospace, automotive, agriculture, and technology sectors.

The Global Supply Chain Management Initiative at Daniels connects students with global companies and industry events throughout the program. The Pathway Program is also available for MSGSCM, allowing Purdue undergraduates and select partner school graduates to streamline directly into the degree. Additionally, Purdue has an agreement with MIT — students who hold an MITx MicroMasters certificate in supply chain management can waive the GMAT requirement for this program.

For a prospect with an engineering, operations, or supply chain undergraduate background, the MSGSCM is often the clearest and highest-ROI recommendation at Daniels, combining strong employer relationships with a favorable labor market and the highest earnings ceiling of any specialized master's program.
