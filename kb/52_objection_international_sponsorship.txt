OBJECTION RESPONSE — "I'M INTERNATIONAL — IS SPONSORSHIP REALISTIC AFTER GRADUATION?"

Underlying concern: The prospect is weighing whether a U.S. graduate degree is worth the investment if they cannot secure long-term work authorization. This is a legitimate and serious concern that deserves honesty, not reassurance-by-default.

How to respond — the frame:

Lead with honesty about what you cannot promise. Visa sponsorship is always an employer-and-role decision, never a program or school decision. Be explicit that no one — not the admissions team, not this agent, not any recruiter — can predict an individual sponsorship outcome. Promising otherwise damages trust and sets the applicant up for disappointment.

Then pivot to what is structurally in their favor. The two concrete structural factors that matter are (1) STEM designation, which determines how long they can work in the U.S. after graduation without employer sponsorship, and (2) employer partner sponsorship behavior, which determines whether the companies recruiting at Daniels are the kinds of employers that sponsor H-1B visas at all. For the factual details on both — which specific programs are STEM-designated, the 36-month OPT window, and the employer partners who are among the largest U.S. H-1B sponsors — retrieve chunk 41 (STEM designation and OPT eligibility).

The combined message the applicant should walk away with: the program and employer ecosystem are structured to give you the best realistic chance of navigating sponsorship, but the final outcome rests with the employer and role. That is the honest answer.

Tone guardrails:

Do not make any promise about H-1B outcomes, timelines, or lottery odds. Do not characterize sponsorship as "likely" or "typical" — use "structurally favorable" or "the best starting position" instead.

Do not recite employer names as if they guarantee sponsorship. The point is that Purdue's employer partners are a sponsorship-capable ecosystem, not that any specific company will sponsor any specific graduate.

For specific visa questions — timing of OPT filing, H-1B cap-gap, STEM extension paperwork, status changes, country-specific considerations — direct the prospect to Purdue's International Students and Scholars office or an immigration attorney. These are the authoritative sources, and this agent is not.

When this objection often surfaces: late in the conversation, after program fit has been established and the applicant is running the cost-benefit math. Treat it as a serious decision-stage concern, not an early-stage filter. If it surfaces very early, it may be a sign the applicant has been burned by prior conversations elsewhere — slow down, acknowledge the concern directly, and move into the structural facts from chunk 41.
