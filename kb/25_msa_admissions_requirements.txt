MS ACCOUNTING (MSA) — ADMISSIONS REQUIREMENTS

The MSA is a one-year residential program. The typical applicant has an undergraduate degree in accounting or a closely related field. Students from other business majors who have a strong interest in moving into accounting are also considered, though prerequisites may apply — contacting the admissions office directly is recommended to confirm whether a background is suitable.

GMAT and GRE test scores are not required for applicants with a cumulative undergraduate GPA of 3.0 or higher. Test scores are encouraged for scholarship consideration and to strengthen a competitive application, but a strong GPA is sufficient for admission eligibility. If a GMAT or GRE has already been taken, scores become part of the application and will be considered.

Work experience is not required for the MSA. Most students enter directly from undergraduate study or within one to two years of graduation. The program is explicitly designed as a post-undergraduate credential for students who want to complete the 150 credit hours required for CPA licensure and move directly into public accounting.

Deadlines for the MSA follow a priority structure. Applying by the January priority deadline maximizes eligibility for merit-based scholarships. Applications submitted after the priority deadline may still be considered for financial aid, but availability is not guaranteed. There are additional deadlines in March and May for students who are not applying in the priority round.

The Pathway Program is available for this degree. Purdue undergraduates who apply through the Pathway Program benefit from waived application components, including the TOEFL or IELTS requirement for U.S. graduates. Students considering the Pathway Program are encouraged to begin planning as early as their sophomore year.

For a prospective student asking whether they can get into the MSA: if they have a 3.0 or above GPA and an accounting or related business background, the path to admission is straightforward. The application is holistic — the Statement of Purpose and recommendations carry significant weight alongside the academic record.
