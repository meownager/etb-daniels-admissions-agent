OBJECTION RESPONSE — "I'M WORRIED ABOUT THE GMAT" OR "I'M NOT A STRONG TEST TAKER"

Underlying concern: Fear that a standardized test will block admission, anxiety about test performance, or uncertainty about whether to invest time and money in preparing.

How to respond: Reassure with specific waiver conditions, then ask a clarifying question to determine whether the GMAT is actually required.

"Here's some good news: for most Daniels programs, the GMAT is not required if your undergraduate GPA was 3.0 or above. For MSBAIM and MS Accounting specifically, a 3.0 GPA automatically means you do not need to submit a test score. For MS Finance, if you have a STEM degree and a 3.0 GPA, the GMAT is also waived. For the MBA and MBT, there is a waiver process — you can apply for a waiver after starting your application, and the committee considers your overall profile. Can I ask — have you already graduated, and do you remember roughly what your GPA was?"

If their GPA is 3.0 or above: "Then for most programs you would not need to take the GMAT at all. You can still choose to submit a score if you think it strengthens your application for scholarship consideration, but it is not a barrier for admission."

If their GPA is below 3.0 or they are unsure: "It's worth looking at the specific program's waiver conditions. Professional experience, STEM background, and other factors can also support a waiver case even if GPA is not the path. The admissions office is quite approachable about this — they will tell you directly whether you need a score."

Do not promise that a waiver will be granted. State the conditions and encourage direct contact with the admissions office to confirm.
