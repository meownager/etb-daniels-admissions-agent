ROI AND PAYBACK PERIOD FRAMING

When a prospect asks whether the investment is worth it, the most useful framing is the payback period — how long after graduation before the salary difference covers the cost of the program.

For MSBAIM graduates: the average starting salary is approximately $104,779. The total tuition for the residential program is approximately $56,700. At average salary, a graduate recovers the full tuition cost in less than seven months of gross earnings. Even accounting for living expenses and the one year out of the workforce, the financial return on the MSBAIM is among the strongest of any graduate business program nationally.

For MS Accounting graduates: the median salary premium for accounting master's graduates over bachelor's-only peers is approximately 17 percent. For a graduate starting at $73,600 compared to a hypothetical $63,000 without the degree, the annual salary difference is approximately $10,600. At that rate, the program pays back in approximately five to six years — not counting the CPA credential value, which itself opens a salary track that accelerates further over time.

For One-Year MBA graduates: a professional moving from, say, $75,000 in an engineering or operations role into a business leadership or consulting role at $95,000 to $115,000 is gaining $20,000 to $40,000 per year. At that annual gain, the $69,000 MBA pays back in approximately two to three years. Critically, the One-Year MBA requires only 11 months out of the workforce — limiting the earnings interruption compared to a two-year program.

For Online MBA graduates: the ROI calculation is different because the student continues earning throughout the program. The 45 percent of online graduates who moved into higher-responsibility roles typically saw salary increases while still employed and enrolled. The payback period for an online program, when no earnings are interrupted, is often shorter than for a residential program with a higher net cost.

The Purdue cost advantage amplifies all of these payback calculations. A student who pays $60,000 at Purdue for outcomes comparable to a student who pays $120,000 at a private school breaks even two to three years sooner. This is a real and meaningful difference in the financial case for Purdue specifically.

When discussing ROI with a prospect, always frame the figure directionally and note that individual results vary. Do not state specific salary projections as guarantees.
