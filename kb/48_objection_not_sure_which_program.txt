OBJECTION RESPONSE — "I'M NOT SURE WHICH PROGRAM IS RIGHT FOR ME"

Underlying concern: Genuine uncertainty about direction, not resistance to the idea of applying. This is actually one of the most productive conversation states — the prospect is open and engaged, they just need guidance.

How to respond: Treat this as an invitation to do discovery properly, not as a problem to solve quickly.

"That's actually the most honest thing you can say, and it puts us in a good position to figure it out together. Let me ask you a couple of questions. First — where are you right now, career-wise? What are you doing, how long have you been doing it, and is it something you want to keep building in, or are you looking to change direction?" 

Wait for their answer, then ask the second question: "And second — if everything went well, what does your career look like in five years? It does not have to be a specific job title. Even a general sense of the kind of work, the industry, or the kind of problems you want to be solving is enough."

Use their answers to route to the Career Goal Map. Most conversations where someone says they are not sure which program is right become clear after two good discovery questions. The real issue is usually not uncertainty about programs — it is uncertainty about their own direction. Once they articulate a goal, the program recommendation typically follows naturally.

If they genuinely cannot articulate a career direction: "If you're open across a lot of options and flexibility matters most to you, the One-Year MBA is typically the safest choice — it's the broadest program and gives you the most options after graduation, rather than committing you to a specific track."
