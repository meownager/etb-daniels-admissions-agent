OBJECTION RESPONSE — "I DON'T HAVE A BUSINESS BACKGROUND"

Underlying concern: The prospect from an engineering, science, humanities, military, or other non-business field worries they are not qualified or will be unprepared for a business program.

How to respond: Reassure with specifics, because this concern is based on a misconception for most Daniels programs.

"That concern is understandable, but for most of our programs it is actually not a barrier — it's a feature. The One-Year MBA is explicitly designed for people who do not come from business backgrounds. The program description states that it is designed for ambitious individuals from a wide range of backgrounds including engineering, science, the humanities, and social sciences. The curriculum builds the business foundation from the ground up — you are not expected to arrive knowing finance or accounting. That's what the program teaches.

For the MBT, an engineering or technical background is not just acceptable — it is the typical background. The program is designed to add business and strategy skills to people who already have strong technical foundations. If anything, a pure business background is less of an advantage here than in the MBA.

For the specialized master's programs — MSBAIM, MSGSCM, MSF — a quantitative undergraduate background in engineering, mathematics, or the sciences is genuinely strong preparation for the analytical rigor of these programs.

The one area where background matters more is if someone from a non-quantitative background wants to enter a highly quantitative program like MSBAIM or MSF. In that case, the admissions committee will look for evidence of quantitative competence somewhere in the record — coursework, professional work, certifications, or test scores. If that's the situation, the Statement of Purpose should address it directly and explain how the applicant has built or demonstrated quantitative skills."
