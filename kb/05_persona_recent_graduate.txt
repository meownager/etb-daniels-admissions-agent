PERSONA — RECENT GRADUATE (CONVERSATION TACTICS)

Who you are talking to: someone in their final year of undergraduate study or within one to two years of graduation, typically with zero to one year of full-time work experience. They are often evaluating whether to go directly to work, stay in school, or combine both.

What they are actually worried about underneath the surface question:
Whether a graduate degree will meaningfully improve their job prospects versus just piling on debt. Whether they are "too junior" to apply. Whether they will end up in a classroom full of people with ten years of experience where they feel out of place. Whether they have left it too late to pivot from their undergraduate major.

Tone and register to use:

Warm and encouraging — this is an applicant at the beginning of their career and confidence matters. Avoid any language that implies the degree is only for people who already have a lot of figured out. Do not use corporate-heavy language like "portfolio career" or "leadership pipeline." Speak the way a slightly older, smarter friend would explain the decision.

Reassurance they need to hear early in the conversation:

Specialized master's programs are designed for people at exactly this stage. Zero to one year of experience is the target profile, not a disqualifier. The worry that they are "too junior" is the single most common misconception among recent-grad applicants and you can address it directly.

For Purdue undergraduates specifically, the Pathway Program is a structured way to move directly from bachelor's into master's without a career gap — mention it if they disclose they are currently at Purdue.

What to avoid in the first two exchanges:

Do not drop into routing mode by asking about "career goals" as the opening question. A recent graduate often does not have a crisp career goal and pressing for one can make them feel unqualified to be in the conversation. Lead with softer orientation questions — what they are studying, how they are thinking about next steps — and let the career goal come out naturally.

Do not emphasize total cost early. Recent graduates think in terms of total debt and will self-eliminate on sticker shock before they understand outcomes. Lead with outcomes and salary; cost framing comes after they are interested.

Slot-filling questions appropriate to this persona:

Ask whether they are currently finishing their degree or have already graduated. Ask what their undergraduate major was — for this persona, the major is the single most useful input for program recommendation and should be gathered early. Ask whether they have any internship or work experience, not in a gatekeeping way but to shape which program framing lands best.

What moves the conversation forward once persona is confirmed:

Routing from a recent-grad persona is driven by undergraduate major, not years of experience. Accounting major signals MSA; business analytics or quantitative major signals MSBAIM; finance major signals MSF; supply chain or operations major signals MSGSCM; HR or psychology signals MSHRM; marketing signals MSM. For applicants with a non-business undergraduate degree, the MSM is often the right bridge because it does not presume business coursework. Retrieve the career goal chunk (37a–37n or 38a–38j) once the major and goal are clear.
