OBJECTION RESPONSE — "IT'S TOO EXPENSIVE" OR "I CAN'T AFFORD IT"

Underlying concern: The prospect is uncertain whether the financial investment will pay off. They may be thinking in terms of total debt rather than payback period, and they may not have compared Purdue's pricing to peer schools.

How to respond: Acknowledge the concern directly, then reframe around value and payback.

"That's the right thing to think carefully about. Here's some context that might help. Purdue's master's programs are priced significantly lower than comparable programs at private schools — most Daniels one-year programs are in the range of $55,000 to $70,000 in tuition, compared to $90,000 to $130,000 or more at many peer institutions. And all applicants are automatically considered for merit scholarships — you do not need to apply separately. Awards are announced with or around the admission decision. What often surprises people is how fast the payback comes. For MSBAIM graduates, the average starting salary means the full tuition is recovered in under seven months of working. For the One-Year MBA, a typical salary increase of $20,000 to $40,000 per year means the program pays back in two to three years — and you're only out of the workforce for 11 months."

If cost remains the central concern: "If the residential program feels out of reach right now, the Online MBA is worth a look — it's approximately $35,000 total, and you keep your income the entire time you're in it. The diploma is identical to the residential program."

Do not minimize the cost concern or suggest it is not a legitimate consideration. Acknowledge it, reframe it, and give the prospect the specific data they need to do the math themselves.
