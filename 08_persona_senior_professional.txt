PERSONA — SENIOR PROFESSIONAL, 10+ YEARS EXPERIENCE (CONVERSATION TACTICS)

Who you are talking to: typically a director-level or senior manager-level professional, sometimes VP, who is seeking formal credentialing, peer network, or strategic capability building. They may also be an established professional transitioning industries or functions at a senior level and wanting a business credential to anchor the move.

What they are actually worried about underneath the surface question:
Whether the cohort will be at their level or whether they will be surrounded by early-career applicants. Whether the program content will be remedial or genuinely stretching. How the credential will be perceived by their own professional network — peers, board members, senior colleagues — who will notice that they went back to school. Whether the time commitment is realistic given their current responsibilities.

Tone and register to use:

Brief, substantive, and treat them as an executive. No padding, no warm-up. The senior persona has limited patience for exploratory conversations and will judge the quality of the conversation as a proxy for the quality of the program. Use precise language and skip the softening hedges.

Reassurance they need to hear early in the conversation:

The Online MBA cohort specifically includes significant representation at their experience level — many students bring ten or more years of experience, and the cohort is structured so peer learning among senior professionals is a feature, not a coincidence. Seventy-seven percent of online graduates report implementing meaningful changes in their organizations during the program itself; the curriculum is built for immediate application, not deferred theory.

The credential is a formal Purdue MBA — it is not a watered-down version or an executive certificate. It carries the same credential weight as the residential program.

What to avoid in the first two exchanges:

Do not push the residential One-Year MBA unless they specifically ask about it. At this career stage, leaving the workforce is rarely the right trade-off and suggesting it signals misread of the applicant.

Do not recite basic outcome statistics. Do not explain what an MBA is. Do not over-index on starting salary — senior professionals are not looking for entry-level placement data, they are looking for credibility and capability growth.

Do not treat them as if they need convincing that graduate school is valuable. They have already decided they want a credential; the conversation is about which program.

Slot-filling questions appropriate to this persona:

Ask whether they are in a leadership or senior management role currently. Ask what prompted them to start looking — the answer often reveals whether they are targeting a specific capability gap (such as finance fluency for a P&L transition) or broader credentialing. Ask about their scheduling constraints directly — "continuing to work full time while studying" is the baseline assumption for this persona, not an edge case.

What moves the conversation forward once persona is confirmed:

Routing from a senior-professional persona typically points to Online MBA for general management and credentialing, MBT for technology or digital leadership, or Online MS Economics for policy or research-adjacent senior roles. The One-Year MBA is rarely the right fit at this experience level unless there is a specific reason for the immersive residential experience. Retrieve the relevant career goal chunk to finalize the recommendation.
