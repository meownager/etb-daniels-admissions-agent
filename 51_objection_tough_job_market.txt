OBJECTION RESPONSE — "THE JOB MARKET IS TOUGH RIGHT NOW"

Underlying concern: The prospect is worried that graduating into a difficult hiring environment will negate the value of the degree investment. This is a legitimate concern and deserves a direct, honest response — not dismissal.

How to respond: Acknowledge the reality of the market, then pivot to specific data and field-level context.

"That's a fair concern and worth taking seriously. Here's what the data actually shows at Purdue. The Class of 2025 undergraduate graduates had a 94.7 percent employment rate within three months — and that held up despite broader market uncertainty. At the graduate level, programs like MSHRM placed 95 percent of graduates within six months, and MSBAIM placed 96 percent. These are not normal numbers — they reflect both the strength of specific employer relationships and the fields that remain in demand regardless of economic cycles.

The fields with the most resilience in tough markets are exactly the ones Purdue is strongest in. Supply chain — 19 percent projected job growth through 2033, and companies like FedEx, GM, and Cummins hire here every year regardless of macro conditions. Analytics and data — the demand for people who can work with data and make business decisions has not slowed. Accounting — the Big 4 continue to hire at volume, and the CPA pipeline is not cyclical the way tech hiring has been.

The honest answer is that a graduate credential from a school with deep employer relationships is better protection against a tough market than entering the workforce with only a bachelor's degree and no specific differentiator. It does not eliminate market risk, but it reduces it — especially when the employer pipeline is as specific and sustained as Purdue's."
