STEM DESIGNATION AND OPT ELIGIBILITY FOR INTERNATIONAL STUDENTS

STEM designation is a formal classification by the U.S. Department of Homeland Security that identifies programs emphasizing science, technology, engineering, or mathematics. For international students on F-1 visas, STEM designation has a direct and significant practical benefit: it extends Optional Practical Training eligibility from 12 months to 36 months total. This means a STEM-designated program graduate can work in the United States for up to three years after graduation under their student visa before requiring employer-sponsored H-1B status.

The following Daniels School programs are STEM-designated: MS Accounting, MSBAIM, MS Finance, MS Global Supply Chain Management, One-Year MBA, MBT (both residential and online), and Online MS Business Analytics. For programs where STEM status is indicated as needing verification — including MSHRM, MS Marketing, Online MBA, and Online MSHRM — prospects should confirm current designation status directly with the admissions office, as program classifications can be updated.

The three-year OPT window is strategically important for international students. It provides meaningful time to establish a professional track record with a U.S. employer, demonstrate value, and navigate the H-1B sponsorship process. Many international graduates use the OPT period to build the professional relationships and employer advocacy needed for a successful H-1B application. The STEM extension is not a guarantee of employment or sponsorship, but it meaningfully increases the time available to secure it.

Purdue's employer partners include many of the largest H-1B sponsors in the United States. Companies like Amazon, Capital One, Goldman Sachs, Cisco, FedEx, and Eli Lilly are consistent Daniels recruiters and are also among the companies that file H-1B petitions regularly. This combination — STEM-designated programs plus employer partners who sponsor — is the most favorable structural setup an international student can have when planning a career in the United States.
