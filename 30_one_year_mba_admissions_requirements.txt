ONE-YEAR MBA — ADMISSIONS REQUIREMENTS

The One-Year MBA is an 11-month full-time residential program. The typical incoming student has two to five years of professional work experience, but there is no strict minimum or maximum. The admissions committee evaluates each application holistically and has admitted students with less experience and with significantly more.

The GMAT or GRE is preferred but not required. A waiver is available — applicants who believe their academic record, professional experience, or other credentials make a strong case without a test score should contact the admissions office to discuss waiver eligibility. Students from engineering, science, or other STEM backgrounds often qualify for a waiver based on the quantitative rigor of their prior coursework. For applicants who do submit scores, the program does not publish a required minimum, but competitive applications typically include scores in a range consistent with the MBA market broadly.

The program explicitly welcomes applicants from non-business backgrounds. Engineers, scientists, military officers, healthcare professionals, and social science graduates have all successfully completed the program and moved into strong business careers. The Statement of Purpose is particularly important for non-business applicants — it should explain clearly why business training is the next logical step given their background and goals, and what they will do with the MBA.

Deadlines are rolling, which means applications are reviewed as they come in rather than in a single review round. Applying earlier in the cycle is advantageous for scholarship consideration and for being considered before cohort seats fill.

The STEM designation of this program is worth noting in the admissions context for international applicants. The STEM designation does not change the application requirements, but it does affect visa planning after graduation — it extends Optional Practical Training eligibility from 12 to 36 months, which is a consideration that many international applicants weigh when choosing between programs.

For a prospective student asking whether they are qualified: if they have two to five years of experience, a clear sense of why they want an MBA, and the ability to articulate their career goals specifically, the application is worth submitting. The holistic review means that a lower GPA can be offset by strong professional experience and a compelling Statement of Purpose.
