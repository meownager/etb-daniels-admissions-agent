CAREER GOAL — ACCOUNTING, AUDIT, TAX, OR CPA TRACK

Primary recommendation: MS Accounting (MSA).

The MSA is the direct path for anyone targeting the CPA, a role in public accounting, corporate finance and accounting, audit, or tax advisory. The program satisfies the 150-credit-hour requirement for CPA certification in most U.S. jurisdictions — this is the original reason the program was developed, and it remains the core structural benefit for CPA-track applicants.

The MSA has strong placement into the Big Four accounting firms, mid-size public accounting firms, and corporate accounting functions at Fortune 500 companies. Graduates also move into audit analytics, forensic accounting, and transaction advisory services. For a career goal that includes or centers on CPA licensure, the MSA removes the biggest structural barrier that applicants without a master's face.

Secondary recommendation: the Pathway Program for current Purdue undergraduate accounting students. The MSA is the original Pathway program for a reason — accounting undergraduates benefit most directly from streamlining into this master's. For a current Purdue accounting student asking about the CPA, the Pathway Program is almost always the clearest path.

Key framing: the MSA is not the right choice for someone who wants to work "in finance" broadly — that is the MSF. The MSA is specifically right when the applicant is targeting accounting, audit, tax, or CPA credentialing. The distinction between finance and accounting is worth clarifying early in the conversation if a prospect is unsure, as the programs serve different career paths despite adjacent vocabulary.

For applicants who want to combine accounting with analytics or data (e.g., audit analytics, internal audit with data focus), the MSBAIM is sometimes a better fit if the analytics orientation is stronger than the CPA motivation. Ask directly: is CPA certification the goal, or is using data in a business context the goal?
